## Open: round 4 (asked, not answered)

### Correction to give before re-asking

Round 4 Q1 claimed a store's commission is "25–60× a collector policy." It relied on a guessed store premium of $2–5k. The store research found **dealer premiums of about $500–1,000 per year for $100k of stock** (2021 data), at about 15% commission, which is **roughly $75–375 per store per year**. That's still about 10–25× the commission on a $10k collector policy ($6–15), but it means **store revenue has to come mainly from the software subscription**. Existing store inventory tools charge **$99–150/month**, which is a useful price anchor. Card-store counts are small: about 1,500–3,000 in the US (EST).

New fact that supports a store-first plan: the CIS dealer policy requires dealers to **keep their own inventory records**. A slab-level inventory tool directly supports their claims.

### Context for Q5: when is a collection too small to insure? (estimates [U])

| Slabs held | Annual premium at 0.5–1% | What's sensible |
|---|---|---|
| Under ~$2,500 | $12–25 | Not worth a standalone policy. Homeowners/renters sublimits roughly cover it, unless the User has no renters insurance. |
| $2,500–$10,000 | $25–100 | Borderline. Carrier minimum premiums may apply. A rider on an existing policy is often cheaper. |
| $10,000+ | $100+ | Standalone coverage makes sense. This is the High-value collector. |

Services for smaller collectors: a sublimit check, Portfolio tracking and price alerts, grading guidance (is a raw card worth grading at PSA's new prices?), cert verification when buying, the Beneficiary list, and a referral to renters insurance or a rider.

### The questions

**Q1 - Phase one is a service for Card stores.** Build phase one around the Store Owner: inventory tracking for the store's slab case (scan cert numbers, see the case's value over time), a commercial coverage quote request handled by hand, and a store-branded sign-up so the store's customers become Users. Collector insurance and Beneficiaries move to phase two, built on the same Portfolio engine. Edge case: most store stock is probably raw singles and sealed product [U], and v1 is slabs only. Is it acceptable that the store tool covers only the slab case?
➡️ Recommended: yes. It's a reasonable first wedge and the Portfolio engine carries into phase two. Store requests for raw and sealed product become v2 evidence. (Restate the commission point using the correction above.)

**Q2 - Making "Market value" fit.** The user proposed "Market value" instead of agreed or estimated value. Proposal: **Market value** = Card Shield's current estimate of what a slab would sell for. The two policy types are renamed **Agreed-value basis** (a fixed Agreed value printed on the policy, which starts from the Market value when the slab is scheduled) and **Market-value basis** (a loss is paid at the Market value on the day of the claim). Drop "Estimated value." Example: a slab with a Market value of $10k is scheduled at an Agreed value of $10k. The market drops 40%, so the Market value falls to $6k. A theft pays $10k on agreed-value basis and $6k on market-value basis.
➡️ Recommended: yes. If accepted, update the Coverage section of GLOSSARY.md.

**Q3 - Should James pitch the brokerage?** (A large, independently owned brokerage.) Order: (1) have a lawyer review James's employment agreement now. An IP-assignment clause covering insurance work could mean the brokerage already has a claim on Card Shield, in which case pitching is how you negotiate a release or partnership. (2) If there's no claim, stay independent until the pilot has data, then pitch the brokerage as a distribution and producer partner, not an owner. It could place the commercial store policies.
➡️ Recommended: yes to that order.

**Q4 - The six-month check-in and salary trigger.** Track four numbers at each check-in: (1) revenue run-rate (last 3 months, annualized), (2) revenue per 1,000 Offered, by channel, (3) hard costs (price data about $100–250/month, hosting, cyber insurance, legal), and (4) stores signed. Salary trigger: the run-rate covers hard costs plus one salary for two check-ins in a row. Stop/pivot line at the 12-month check-in; the user must set that number.
➡️ Recommended: yes to the structure. Set the stop/pivot number at the first check-in.

**Q5 - What smaller collectors get.** Don't sell standalone policies below about $2,500 of slabs. Smaller collectors get the sublimit check, tracking, grading guidance and cert verification as the free tier. Policies are offered from about $2,500 up, wherever partners' minimum premiums allow.
➡️ Recommended: yes. In phase one, store customers who sign up through the store land here.
